# Lip Fillers Aftercare: 7 Things to Do (and 5 to Avoid) in the First 48 Hours

You've just left the clinic. Your lips feel a little tender, possibly a little puffy, and you're already wondering if you should be doing something differently. The good news — most of what helps your results in the first two days is simple. Knowing exactly what's normal (and what isn't) takes the anxiety out of the swelling, the asymmetry, and that one tiny bruise that always lands in the most visible place.

This is the practical [lip fillers](/treatments/lip-fillers/) aftercare guide we give every client at KESHO Aesthetics in Finchley.

## The first 48 hours matter more than you think

Lip filler results are made in the treatment room, but they're protected (or sabotaged) in the two days that follow. The choices you make about sleep, exercise, alcohol, heat and what touches your lips during this window influence three things: how much you swell, how much you bruise, and how cleanly the product integrates with your tissue.

You don't need to wrap yourself in cotton wool. You do need to be a little more thoughtful than usual.

## What's actually happening under the skin during this window

When Claire or Kyle injects hyaluronic acid (HA) filler into your lips, several things happen at once. Tiny entry points create micro-trauma. The product begins drawing water to itself — which is why swelling peaks at 24-72 hours, not immediately. Small blood vessels may have been nudged, producing bruises that bloom over the next day or two.

By 48 hours, the worst of the swelling typically subsides. By day 7, most people look very close to their final result. At 14 days, the filler has fully settled — which is why we book a complimentary review at that point.

## The 7 things to DO

### 1. Apply cold compresses (15 min on, 15 off — first 24h)

Cold is your best friend on day one. It constricts blood vessels (less bruising), calms inflammation (less swelling) and numbs minor tenderness. Use a clean gel pack or a wrapped bag of frozen peas — never ice directly on the skin. Fifteen minutes on, fifteen off, for the first 24 hours whenever you're sitting still. Press gently — this isn't the moment to mould your lips.

### 2. Sleep elevated on your back the first night

Gravity is doing a job whether you help it or not. Lying flat — or worse, face-down on a pillow — pools fluid in your lips and amplifies morning swelling. Prop yourself up with an extra pillow and aim to sleep on your back for the first night. If you're a committed side-sleeper, two pillows and a determined effort will do.

### 3. Hydrate

HA filler works by attracting water, and dehydrated tissue heals more slowly. Aim for two litres across the day, and keep a glass by the bed. Use a straw sparingly in the first 24 hours — the pursing motion isn't ideal, but a few sips won't ruin anything.

### 4. Eat soft foods

For the first day, treat your lips like a fresh tattoo — minimal disturbance. Soups, smoothies, scrambled eggs, pasta, soft fish. Skip the burger that requires a full mouth-open assault and the crusty sourdough that drags across your lips. This is about avoiding pressure and friction while entry points are still settling.

### 5. Use clean lip balm sparingly after 24h

After the first 24 hours, a thin layer of plain, fragrance-free balm can soothe any dryness. Squeeze it onto a clean fingertip rather than applying the tube directly to your lips. Avoid anything medicated, tingling or flavoured for the first week.

### 6. Take paracetamol if needed (NOT ibuprofen or aspirin first 24h)

Most clients don't need pain relief beyond a mild ache. If you do, paracetamol is the right choice. Ibuprofen, aspirin and other NSAIDs thin the blood and meaningfully increase bruising — avoid them for the first 24 hours. The same applies to fish oil, high-dose vitamin E and ginkgo supplements if you take them regularly.

### 7. Be patient — final shape settles at 2 weeks

This is the hardest one. At 24 hours, your lips will likely look bigger than expected. At 72 hours, they may look uneven. At day 7, you'll start seeing the real shape. At 14 days, what you see is what you've got. Don't judge the result — or, with affection, text your practitioner a panicked photo on day three. Give it the full fortnight.

## The 5 things to AVOID

### 1. Touching, pressing, or massaging (unless specifically guided)

Your hands carry bacteria, and your lips have fresh micro-injuries. Beyond infection risk, repeated pressing can displace product before it integrates. The exception: if your practitioner has shown you a specific massage technique for a particular reason, follow that instruction exactly. Otherwise — hands off.

### 2. Strenuous exercise for 24-48h

Raised heart rate means raised blood pressure, which means more swelling and bruising. Walking is fine. Yin yoga is fine. Spin, HIIT, heavy lifting, hot yoga and long runs — leave them for 48 hours. Many clients book a Friday afternoon for exactly this reason.

### 3. Alcohol for 24h

Alcohol dilates blood vessels, dehydrates you, and dramatically increases bruising. Ideally avoid it for 24 hours before and 24 hours after treatment. If you have a non-negotiable event in the window, mention it when you [book](/book/) so we can plan timing accordingly.

### 4. Heat (sauna, steam, hot showers) for 48h

Heat does the same thing alcohol does — dilates vessels and increases swelling. Skip saunas, steam rooms, sunbeds, very hot baths and long hot showers for 48 hours. A warm shower with your face out of the direct stream is fine.

### 5. Lipstick / makeup on lips for 24h

Lipsticks, glosses and liners carry bacteria, and the applicators have touched your lips before. Give entry points 24 hours to close before reintroducing any product. After that, use a fresh tube or a freshly cleaned brush for the first week.

## What's normal — the swelling, bruising, asymmetry timeline

Knowing the timeline takes the fear out of the mirror.

- **Hours 0-24:** Initial tenderness, mild swelling, possible pinpoint bruises at entry sites. Lips may feel firmer than usual.
- **Day 1-3:** Peak swelling. Lips often look larger and rounder than the final result. Bruises may darken before they fade.
- **Day 4-7:** Swelling subsides noticeably. Bruising shifts to yellow-green as it resolves. Shape begins to look like you.
- **Day 7-14:** Subtle integration. Any small lumps you can feel (not see) usually soften and disappear. Final shape emerges.
- **Day 14:** Settled result. This is your review point.

Mild asymmetry in the first week is the rule, not the exception — one side almost always swells more than the other. Trust the timeline before you trust the mirror.

## What's NOT normal — when to call us

Please read this carefully, then save our number.

The rare but serious complication to know about is **vascular occlusion** — when filler compresses or enters a blood vessel and interrupts blood supply to the tissue. It is uncommon when treatment is performed by trained, experienced practitioners using safe technique, but every client deserves to know the warning signs.

Call us immediately — not tomorrow, not after work — if you experience any of the following:

- **Severe, escalating pain** that is not relieved by cold compresses or paracetamol, particularly if it worsens over hours rather than improves
- **Blanching** — white or pale patches on the lip or surrounding skin
- **Mottled, dusky, purplish or grey discolouration** that doesn't look like a normal bruise (bruises are localised; vascular changes spread)
- **Severe coolness** of the area compared to surrounding skin
- **Vision changes, severe headache, or skin changes beyond the lip** — extremely rare with lip treatment, but call without hesitation

This is a medical emergency. The treatment is hyaluronidase — an enzyme that dissolves HA filler — and Kyle, as a GPhC-registered Independent Prescribing Pharmacist, can dispense and administer it on-site. Time matters: call first. Use the number on our [contact page](/contact/) and tell us what you're seeing.

Less urgent but still worth a call: a lump that's still firm after two weeks; bruising that hasn't shifted at all by day 10; any sign of infection (spreading redness, heat, pus, fever). We'd always rather hear from you and reassure you than have you sit at home worrying.

## FAQ

**Can I drink coffee after lip filler?**
Yes — caffeine isn't restricted. Just let very hot drinks cool slightly for the first 24 hours.

**Can I fly after lip filler?**
Short-haul is generally fine after 24 hours, though cabin pressure can temporarily increase swelling. For long-haul, we'd suggest leaving 5-7 days where possible.

**When can I kiss?**
Keep it gentle for the first 24-48 hours to avoid pressure on entry points.

**Will I have lumps?**
Small, firm spots you can feel but not see are common in the first week and usually soften. Any lump still present at the 2-week review is something we can address — often with gentle massage, occasionally with a tiny amount of hyaluronidase.

**How soon can I get a top-up if I want more volume?**
Always wait for the 2-week review first. Adding more before the filler has settled is how lips end up overdone.

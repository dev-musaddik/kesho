# Microneedling with PDRN, Collagen or Exosomes — Explained

Microneedling used to be a simple conversation: a roller or pen, a little downtime, glowier skin a week later. It is a different proposition now. The devices have improved, but the bigger shift is what we put through those tiny channels afterwards — PDRN, collagen and now exosomes. Each one changes the result, the price and the type of skin it suits.

If you have been quoted three very different numbers at three different clinics, this is the post for you. We will explain what is actually happening to your skin, what each booster does, and how to choose the right one.

## Microneedling 101: what is actually happening to your skin

A medical-grade microneedling pen uses a cluster of very fine, sterile needles that move up and down at high speed, creating thousands of microscopic, controlled injuries. The channels are smaller than a human hair and close within hours.

What matters is the response. Your skin reads those tiny injuries as a signal to repair, and the repair process is the treatment. Fibroblasts produce new collagen and elastin, blood supply increases, cell turnover accelerates. Over the following weeks, the skin you grow back is firmer, smoother and more even.

We typically work between 0.5mm and 2.5mm — shallower for delicate areas like the under-eye, deeper for textural concerns like acne scarring on the cheeks. Depth is one lever; the topical product driven into those channels is the other.

You can read more about how we deliver the treatment itself on our [microneedling treatment page](/treatments/microneedling/).

## Why the "topical" matters as much as the needle depth

For a brief window after microneedling, your skin is unusually receptive. The channels are open and the barrier that normally keeps molecules out is temporarily bypassed. Anything we apply can travel deeper and reach the layers where it actually does its work, rather than sitting on the surface.

This is why a microneedling session today is rarely just microneedling. The needle creates access; the booster does the heavy lifting. A collagen serum will hydrate and plump. PDRN will calm and regenerate. Exosomes will signal a much wider repair response. Same needle, very different result — which is also why price differences between clinics make sense once you look at what is being used.

## PDRN microneedling explained

PDRN stands for polydeoxyribonucleotide — small fragments of DNA, sourced from purified salmon, that share enough similarity to human DNA to act as a biological messenger in your skin. It has years of clinical use behind it, particularly in South Korea and across Europe.

PDRN does two things very well. It calms inflammation — which makes it our go-to for sensitive, reactive or post-acne skin — and it stimulates tissue regeneration through a receptor pathway called A2A. In practice that means faster healing, less redness over time, softer post-acne scarring and a more even tone.

It is the right choice if your skin runs warm, breaks out, scars easily, or simply does not heal as quickly as it used to. Patients with a history of acne often see the biggest visual change with PDRN because it addresses both texture and the underlying inflammatory tendency. The Rejuran range is probably the most recognisable PDRN brand in the UK.

## Collagen microneedling explained

Collagen microneedling is the most established of the three, and for many people it is still the right answer. We use a hydrolysed collagen preparation — collagen broken into fragments small enough to pass through the channels and reach the dermis where it can be useful.

What it does well: hydration, plumping, softening of fine lines, an overall freshness. What it does not do as dramatically as the other two: heavy regenerative work on scarring or deep ageing changes. It is a lovely "first microneedling" for someone in their late twenties or thirties whose main complaint is dullness, dehydration or early fine lines, at an accessible price point.

If hydration and glow are your priority, collagen often does the job. It also pairs well with our wider [skin boosters](/treatments/skin-boosters/) range if you want to layer treatments through the year.

## Exosome microneedling explained

Exosomes are the newest and currently the most powerful regenerative option we offer. They are tiny vesicles — microscopic delivery packages — extracted from stem cells. In the UK, the exosomes we use are plant-derived, which sidesteps the regulatory and ethical issues attached to human or animal-derived sources.

What is inside those vesicles is the interesting part: a cocktail of growth factors, peptides and signalling molecules. When you drive exosomes into freshly microneedled skin, they do not simply add hydration or a single active. They tell your cells how to behave better — repair faster, produce more collagen, calm inflammatory signalling, behave like younger versions of themselves.

This is why microneedling exosomes treatments are the strongest option for visibly ageing skin, stubborn pigmentation, post-procedure recovery and — applied to the scalp — early hair thinning. The trade-off is straightforward: exosomes cost more because the product itself is more expensive to manufacture. For the right candidate, the difference in outcome justifies the difference in price.

## Side-by-side: which booster for which concern

Rather than ranking them, it helps to match them to the problem you are actually trying to solve.

- **Dullness, dehydration, early fine lines, "first microneedling"** — Collagen. The accessible entry point, very respectable results, easy to build into an annual rhythm.
- **Sensitive or reactive skin, redness, post-acne marks, dehydrated and inflamed skin** — PDRN. Calms and regenerates simultaneously, which is rare.
- **Visible ageing, deeper textural change, pigmentation, post-procedure recovery, hair thinning on the scalp** — Exosomes. The biggest regenerative push currently available in clinic.

You are not locked in. A common pattern at KESHO is to start with PDRN to settle the skin, then move to exosomes once it is calm and ready for more aggressive regeneration. Equally, someone with straightforward goals may stay on collagen for years and never need to upgrade. The right answer depends on your skin, not on the price list.

## Realistic results timeline (when to expect what)

Expectations are where most disappointment with microneedling comes from, so it is worth being precise.

**Days 1 to 3.** Skin looks pink, feels tight, possibly a little sandpapery. Makeup goes back on at 24 hours.

**Week 1 to 2.** The post-treatment glow appears. Skin looks fresher, fine lines softer, hydration visibly better. This is the surface result — real, but not yet the structural one.

**After session 2 (weeks 5 to 8).** Most patients see a tangible difference: tone more even, pores smaller, texture settled. This is usually the point where people decide microneedling is worth continuing.

**3 months after your final session.** The deeper collagen remodelling completes. This is the result we are aiming for, and the result to judge the course against — not the photo the day after session one.

PDRN and exosomes accelerate and amplify this timeline. Collagen results are more gradual but follow the same shape.

## What microneedling can — and cannot — do

A treatment is only as good as the problem it is matched to, so it is worth being clear about scope.

**Microneedling can** improve skin texture, soften fine lines, reduce the appearance of pores, fade post-inflammatory pigmentation, improve acne scarring, and create a genuine luminosity that no topical product can match on its own.

**Microneedling cannot** replace dermal filler — it does not add structural volume to cheeks, lips or temples. It cannot replace anti-wrinkle injections — it does not relax the muscles that create dynamic frown lines or crow's feet. If your main concern is volume loss or movement-driven lines, microneedling is a supporting treatment, not the lead one.

We will tell you if microneedling is not the right starting point for your goals. It is one of the most-loved treatments in the clinic, but it is not magic, and it is not the answer to every concern.

## Aftercare essentials

The first 24 hours matter more than the first week.

- No makeup for 24 hours. After that, mineral makeup is fine.
- No active skincare for 5 to 7 days — that means retinoids, vitamin C, acids, scrubs. Stick to a gentle cleanser, a bland moisturiser and SPF.
- No hot showers, saunas, steam rooms, intense exercise or sun exposure for 48 hours.
- Daily SPF 50 from day two onwards, without exception. Freshly remodelled skin pigments very easily.
- Drink water, sleep well, leave the skin alone. Picking or exfoliating during the healing window is the fastest way to undo the work.

We will give you a written aftercare card on the day so nothing is left to memory.

## FAQ

**Does microneedling hurt?**
We apply a numbing cream for 20 to 30 minutes before treatment. Most patients describe it as a strong vibration rather than pain. Bonier areas like the forehead and jawline are more noticeable than the cheeks.

**How long is the downtime?**
Realistically, one to two days of looking flushed and slightly tight. Many patients return to work the next day. Sensitive skins may stay pink for three to four days.

**Can I have microneedling if I have active acne?**
Active inflammatory breakouts are a reason to delay, because we do not want to spread bacteria across the face. Post-acne scarring and marks are some of the best indications for microneedling with PDRN.

**Is exosome microneedling safe?**
The plant-derived exosomes we use in the UK have a strong safety profile. As with any treatment, your suitability is assessed at consultation.

**How often should I have microneedling?**
A course of three sessions, four to six weeks apart, then a maintenance session every three to six months depending on your skin and goals.

**Which booster do you recommend for hair thinning?**
Exosomes applied to the scalp, alongside microneedling, is our preferred protocol for early-stage hair thinning. This is assessed separately at consultation.

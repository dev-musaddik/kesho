# The KESHO Approach to Natural Results: Our Treatment Philosophy

Most clinic websites have a page like this, and most say the same thing: bespoke, natural, expert, safe. The words are correct. They have also been used so often, by so many clinics doing such different work, that they have stopped meaning much.

So this is not a values page. It is an attempt to explain — honestly — how we think about aesthetic treatment at KESHO, what we will and will not do, and why a clinic in Finchley would rather lose a booking than give someone a result they will regret in twelve weeks.

If you are nervous about looking "done" or like a slightly different version of yourself, this is the piece worth reading before you book anything.

## Why "elevate" — and not "transform" or "rejuvenate"

Our tagline is *Elevate Your Beauty*. We chose the word carefully.

*Transform* implies you need to become someone else. *Rejuvenate* implies a clock to turn back. Both promise something aesthetic medicine cannot really deliver, and both miss the point of what a good treatment is supposed to do.

Elevate is quieter. It assumes the foundation is already there. The work is to lift it — to soften an edge that has been bothering you, to restore proportion that has shifted, to refresh skin that has lost some of its glow. You should still look like yourself in every photograph your friends take of you. Your colleagues should not be able to put their finger on what has changed. Your reflection should just feel a little more like the version of you that lives in your head.

That is the bar. Anything else, in our view, is the wrong job.

## The Instagram-face problem (and why we don't chase it)

There is a recognisable face circulating in aesthetic medicine right now. Lifted cheeks that catch the light at unnatural angles. Lips with a particular tented shape. A jawline that does not quite match the rest of the face. It is not one person. It is a composite — a beauty ideal flattened by filters and front-facing cameras, then re-engineered onto real faces by clinics willing to oblige.

We are not interested in that face. Not because it is wrong to want a strong jawline or fuller lips — those are perfectly reasonable goals — but because the composite ignores the individual. A cheek projection that works on a long, slim face looks heavy on a round one. A lip shape that flatters one mouth distorts another. Copying a look from a screen onto a face that is structurally different is how patients end up looking, in the unfortunate phrase, "windswept".

A natural-looking aesthetic treatment starts from the opposite premise. Not "what does the trend say?" but "what does this specific face need, and what is the smallest, most considered intervention that delivers it?"

## Three principles guiding every KESHO treatment

We do not have a long list of internal rules. We have three.

### Principle 1: We treat you, not your insecurity

Patients often arrive having decided what they need. Cheek filler. A lip flip. Jawline contouring. Sometimes they are exactly right. Often, the thing they have fixated on is a symptom of something else — tired-looking skin reading as "old" cheeks, or poor sleep registering as under-eye hollows that filler will make worse, not better.

Our job is to look at the whole face, listen to what is actually bothering you, and offer the treatment that addresses it. Sometimes that is not the treatment you walked in asking for. Sometimes it is no treatment at all.

### Principle 2: Less is almost always more

You can always add. It is much harder to take away.

We dose conservatively, particularly on first treatments. We would rather under-treat slightly, see you at a two-week review, and add a touch more — than over-treat on day one and ask you to live with it for nine months. This is not timidity. It is the only sensible way to work in a medium that is, by nature, semi-permanent.

This applies to filler volumes, anti-wrinkle dosing, and the number of treatments we will recommend at one appointment. If someone presents with three things they would like addressed, we will usually treat one or two well rather than all three thinly.

### Principle 3: Reversibility matters

We use hyaluronic acid (HA) fillers almost exclusively. They are not the only option on the market — there are longer-lasting, "biostimulator" alternatives that some clinics favour because the results persist for years.

We choose HA because if something goes wrong — vascular occlusion, asymmetry, a result the patient simply does not like — HA can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase. The longer-lasting alternatives largely cannot. That difference matters more than the marginal convenience of a result that lasts twice as long. The downside risk is not symmetrical, and a clinic taking your safety seriously should not pretend otherwise.

## The consultation: where we say "no" or "wait" more often than you'd expect

Our consultations are complimentary and, deliberately, untethered from any decision to treat. You can sit down, talk through what is bothering you, get a real assessment from Claire — our Lead Aesthetic Practitioner — or one of the clinical team, and walk out without booking anything. A fair number of people do. That is by design.

We say "no" or "not yet" reasonably often. A few examples:

- Someone in their early twenties wanting lip filler when their lips are already well-proportioned. We will usually suggest waiting, or a much smaller volume than they were planning.
- Someone wanting cheek filler when the real issue is skin quality. Skincare and a course of skin treatments will do more for them than 2ml of filler will.
- Someone with mild concerns who would genuinely benefit from a good SPF and three months of a decent retinoid before we discuss anything injectable.
- Someone whose previous treatment elsewhere is still settling, where the right answer is to wait rather than stack new product on top of old.

This is not a sales technique. It is the only honest position. A clinic that says yes to everything is, by definition, not really assessing anyone.

## Product choice: the unglamorous detail that separates clinics

Patients rarely ask which specific product a clinic is using. They should.

We use established, well-studied brands — the same families of HA fillers and botulinum toxin products you will find at every reputable clinic in central London. We do not buy from grey-market suppliers, we do not use unbranded or rebranded product, and we will tell you the exact name and batch number of anything we inject. It is written on your record.

This sounds obvious. In an industry where product can be ordered online by anyone with a credit card and a postcode, it is not. Cheap aesthetics is almost always cheap because the product is, the practitioner is undertrained, or both.

Kyle, our Independent Prescribing Pharmacist, oversees the prescribing side of the clinic, and Dr Ryan Marni, our GMC-registered doctor, provides further medical oversight. None of this is glamorous. All of it matters.

## The slow-build approach (vs. one-shot results)

A good aesthetic outcome is rarely the result of one appointment. It is the result of a sensible plan, executed in small steps, reviewed honestly between each one.

Someone starting filler with us might have a small amount of product placed, return for review at two weeks, sit with the result for three months, and only then decide whether more is needed. Skin treatments work the same way — a course of six over several months will do more than one heroic session ever could.

This is unglamorous and does not photograph well. It is also how you end up looking, three years later, like a slightly better-rested version of yourself rather than someone who has obviously had work.

## Letting the face age — not pause

We are not in the business of stopping ageing. We do not think that is a coherent goal, and we are sceptical of clinics that imply it is.

Faces change. Bone remodels. Fat pads shift. Skin loses elasticity. Aesthetic medicine can soften the visible markers of those changes meaningfully — but it cannot freeze a face in amber. The patients who age most gracefully tend to be the ones who accept that and work with their face over time, rather than chasing a version of themselves from a decade ago.

The job is to help the current version of yourself look the way you feel inside. Not younger, necessarily. Just well.

## What "natural-looking" actually means at KESHO

Natural-looking is a phrase used by every clinic in the country, which is precisely why it has lost most of its meaning. For us, a natural-looking aesthetic treatment passes four tests:

1. **You still look like you in unposed photos** — including the unflattering ones taken at family gatherings.
2. **People comment on how you look, not on what you have had done.** "You look well" rather than "your lips look great".
3. **Your face moves.** Expressions read clearly. You can frown if you want to. You can smile fully.
4. **The result fits the face it is on.** Proportions look proportional. Nothing is fighting with anything else.

If a treatment cannot pass those tests, we would rather not do it. You can read more about [who we are and how we work](/about/) on our team page, or browse the full range of [treatments we offer](/treatments/) to get a sense of what fits within this approach.

## Why we offer a 2-week review on every filler treatment

Filler settles. Swelling resolves. The face you see immediately after treatment is not the face you will see in a fortnight, and assessing the result on day one is genuinely unhelpful.

We book every filler patient in for a complimentary review at around two weeks. This is when we look properly — assess symmetry, check the projection, see how the product has integrated — and, if needed, add a small top-up. The point is that the review is built into the price and the plan from the start, so neither of us is guessing.

It also closes the loop. We see our own work at the point where it matters, learn from it, and adjust how we treat you next time.

## The KESHO promise

We are a business. We will not pretend otherwise. A clinic that does not make money does not stay open, and a clinic that does not stay open cannot look after its patients.

But the business model only holds together if results are good. Aesthetic medicine lives or dies on word of mouth — a patient who looks overfilled tells everyone, and a patient who looks well does the same. Our financial incentives and your aesthetic ones are, genuinely, aligned. The clinics that lose sight of that do not last.

So the promise is straightforward. We will tell you when a treatment will not fix what is bothering you. We will tell you when less is the right answer. We will use products we trust, dose conservatively, review properly, and treat your face as if it were the only one we see this week.

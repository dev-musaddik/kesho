# Dermal Fillers vs Skin Boosters: Which Treatment Is Right For You?

It's one of the most common questions we hear at our Finchley clinic. Someone has been researching their options online, they've seen friends getting "tweakments", and now they're trying to work out whether they need filler, a skin booster, or something else entirely. The marketing makes it harder, not easier — both treatments are often lumped together as "injectables", both use hyaluronic acid, and both get described with the same hopeful adjectives like "natural" and "refreshed".

So let's settle this properly. The dermal fillers vs skin boosters debate isn't really a debate at all once you understand what each treatment is actually designed to do. They are not competitors. They are tools that solve different problems.

## They're both injected. They're both based on hyaluronic acid. They do completely different things.

Here is the source of nearly all the confusion. Both treatments use hyaluronic acid (HA), a sugar molecule your body already produces. Both are delivered via fine needles. Both are temporary. On paper, they sound like variations of the same idea.

In practice, they're as different as a structural beam and a coat of paint. One holds the building up. The other makes the surface look good. You can absolutely have both — most of our clients over 35 do — but they answer different questions about your face.

The simplest way to think about it:

- **Dermal fillers** restore *structure* and *volume*. They lift, contour, and replace what time, gravity and fat loss have taken away.
- **Skin boosters** improve *skin quality* itself. They hydrate, smooth, and rebuild collagen at the level of the skin, without changing your shape.

Once that distinction clicks, the rest of the decision gets much easier.

## What dermal fillers actually do — structural support

[Dermal fillers](/treatments/dermal-fillers/) are made from a thicker, more cross-linked form of hyaluronic acid. "Cross-linked" simply means the HA molecules are bonded together so the gel holds its shape under the skin. That's the whole point — filler is designed to occupy space.

As we age, we lose facial fat pads, our bone resorbs slightly, and the soft tissue of the face descends. The cheeks flatten. The jawline softens. The chin pulls back. Folds form between the nose and mouth (nasolabial folds) and from the corners of the mouth downwards (marionette lines). None of this is about skin quality — it's about lost architecture.

Filler restores that architecture. Placed by a trained practitioner along specific anatomical points, it can:

- Rebuild cheek projection
- Define a softening jawline
- Bring a recessed chin forward into balance
- Lift the midface, which often softens nasolabial folds indirectly
- Restore symmetry after asymmetric volume loss

It is, fundamentally, a sculpting treatment. A good filler result doesn't look "filled" — it looks like your face from a few years ago, before things shifted.

## What skin boosters actually do — skin quality

[Skin boosters](/treatments/skin-boosters/) use a much thinner, less cross-linked formulation of hyaluronic acid (or, in the case of polynucleotides, a different active ingredient altogether). Because they aren't designed to hold a shape, they're delivered as microdroplets spread evenly across an area — typically the cheeks, neck, décolletage, or the back of the hands.

The job isn't to change your contours. It's to change the skin itself. Skin boosters work by:

- Drawing water into the dermis, dramatically improving hydration from within
- Stimulating fibroblasts (the cells that make collagen and elastin)
- Improving elasticity and bounce
- Refining texture and reducing the look of fine, crepey lines
- Producing what people generally describe as "glow"

If your complaint is "my skin looks dull, tired, dehydrated, or papery", that's a booster conversation. If your complaint is "my face has lost its shape", that's a filler conversation.

At Kesho we offer several boosters, because no single product suits every skin:

- **Classic HA skin boosters** — the foundational hydration treatment
- **Skinvive** — a newer HA product designed specifically for the cheeks, with a longer-lasting hydration effect
- **Polynucleotides** — derived from purified salmon or trout DNA fragments, these don't just hydrate, they actively stimulate skin regeneration and repair. A category in its own right
- **Vitamin B12 boosters** — a brightening, energising course often used as a top-up between heavier treatments

On the surface, a single skin booster session can look comparable to a filler treatment. The difference is in how they're used. Filler is typically a one-off treatment that you maintain every 12–18 months. Skin boosters almost always require a **course** — usually three sessions, two to four weeks apart, then a maintenance session every few months.

So while a single polynucleotide session is £250, the realistic first-year investment for a meaningful result is closer to three sessions plus maintenance. Filler, by contrast, is a higher upfront cost but a longer interval between top-ups.

Neither is "cheaper" in absolute terms — they buy different things over different timeframes.

## Longevity: how long each lasts

This is the other major practical difference.

- **Dermal fillers**: typically 12–18 months, sometimes longer in areas with less movement (cheeks, chin) and shorter in areas with more (lips, perioral). The product gradually breaks down via your body's own enzymes.
- **Skin boosters**: typically 4–9 months depending on the product. HA boosters sit at the shorter end. Polynucleotides and Skinvive tend to last longer because they stimulate biological change in the skin rather than just adding hydration.

Filler is the "big project, less often" treatment. Boosters are the "smaller project, more regularly" treatment. Some clients prefer the rhythm of frequent, lower-intensity sessions; others would rather do one larger treatment and forget about it for a year. Both are valid.

## Treatment experience: needles, discomfort, downtime compared

Both treatments involve injections. Beyond that, the experience differs.

**Dermal fillers** are usually placed at deeper levels — sometimes on the bone — through a small number of injection points, often using a blunt-tipped cannula rather than a needle for safety and comfort. Modern fillers contain lidocaine, so the area numbs as we work. Most clients describe pressure rather than pain. Downtime is typically 24–72 hours of mild swelling, occasional bruising at injection points, and the possibility of feeling slightly firm or "settled-in" for a week or two.

**Skin boosters** involve more injection points but at a much more superficial level. The needle is finer, the depth is shallower, the product is thinner. Discomfort is generally lower per injection but the multiple-point delivery means a slightly more "prickly" overall experience. Immediately after, you'll typically see small raised bumps across the treated area — these are the microdroplets of product and they settle completely within a few hours. Some redness and pinpoint bruising is normal. Most people are presentable the same day, fully recovered within 24–48 hours.

Neither treatment requires you to take time off work, but if you have a major event, we'd schedule filler at least two weeks ahead and boosters at least three to five days ahead.

## Choosing between them — by concern

If you're still weighing it up, here's how we'd think about the most common reasons people come in.

**"My cheeks look flat or my jawline isn't defined any more."**
This is filler territory. Boosters won't restore lost structure no matter how many sessions you have.

**"My skin looks dull and dehydrated, especially in winter."**
This is a booster conversation, almost always. Filler won't make your skin glow.

**"I look tired all the time."**
This needs assessment. It could be under-eye volume loss (filler, very carefully), midface flattening casting shadows (filler), or skin that's lost its reflective quality (boosters). Often both contribute.

**"I want to age well — I'm in my mid-30s and want to stay ahead of things."**
This is overwhelmingly a booster conversation. At this stage, maintaining collagen and hydration tends to give better long-term results than adding volume you haven't yet lost. Polynucleotides in particular have become a strong choice for this group.

**"I've lost weight and my face has changed."**
Filler-led, often combined with later booster sessions to address the skin laxity that follows fat loss.

**"My neck and décolletage are showing my age more than my face."**
Boosters — particularly polynucleotides or HA skin boosters — were practically invented for this.

## Why many people benefit from BOTH (sequenced, not combined in one session)

This is the part of the conversation that surprises people. The honest answer to "which one do I need?" is, for many clients over 35, *eventually both*.

Filler restores the architecture. Boosters maintain the surface. They work on different layers of the face and they age it back in different ways. A face with beautifully restored cheek structure but dull, dehydrated skin still looks tired. A face with luminous, well-hydrated skin but no underlying support still looks deflated.

We don't combine them in a single session. Filler is placed and then needs time to integrate and settle — usually two to four weeks before we'd do anything else in the same area. Boosters, similarly, need their initial inflammatory response to settle before further work. Sequencing matters, and a good practitioner will map this out across several months rather than try to do everything at once.

A typical Kesho sequence for a new client in their 40s might look like:

1. Consultation and assessment
2. Filler where structurally needed (often cheeks or jawline)
3. Two to three weeks later, begin a skin booster course
4. Skin booster sessions two to four weeks apart
5. Review at the three-month mark and plan maintenance

Not everyone needs this. Many people only need one or the other for years. The point is that the two treatments are complementary, not competitive.

## FAQ

**Can I have dermal fillers and skin boosters in the same area?**
Yes, but not in the same session. We typically place filler first, allow two to four weeks for it to integrate, then begin a booster course.

**Will skin boosters change the shape of my face?**
No. They are not designed to add volume in any meaningful way. If you're hoping for a shape change, you need filler.

**Are polynucleotides a type of skin booster?**
They're often grouped with skin boosters because they're used similarly, but they're a different active ingredient. Rather than hydrating the skin, they stimulate the skin's own regenerative cells. We consider them their own category.

**Which lasts longer?**
Filler — typically 12–18 months versus 4–9 months for boosters.

**Which is more painful?**
Filler involves fewer, deeper injections with built-in numbing. Boosters involve more, shallower injections with topical numbing. Most clients find both very tolerable.

**Can either be reversed?**
Hyaluronic acid filler can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase. Skin boosters don't generally need reversing because they sit in microdroplets that the body absorbs.

**I'm in my late 20s — do I need either?**
You probably don't need filler. You may benefit from a light booster course to maintain skin quality, particularly if you have a demanding lifestyle, but this is a conversation rather than a default recommendation.

**Is one better value than the other?**
They buy different things. Filler is higher upfront, less frequent. Boosters are lower per session but require a course and more regular maintenance. Neither is inherently better value — only the right treatment for the right concern is.
